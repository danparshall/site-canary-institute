P014 Design-flow and fab-process integrity

What detection rate do automated trojan detectors reach on design-flow and fab-process data for unseen trojan families at accelerator scale?

Trojan detection in design flow and fab process data.

Status

Analyzed A desk analysis of the fab-process clause: repurposing fabs' existing process-data anomaly detection and virtual metrology for security, with its stated limits (catches only attacks that show as statistical deviations; high false-positive risk in a noisy fab). No detection rate anywhere. The design-flow clause rests on graph-neural-network detectors trained on netlists reported second-hand (Thorat et al. 2025, not held), which flag patterns resembling known Trojans rather than unseen families; no held document tests any detector at accelerator scale or on unseen trojan families. Logic locking and other obfuscation are described in the same paper as broken by SAT-style attacks, but those attack papers are not held.

A more scalable approach is to integrate verification into the fabrication process itself. Modern fabs already collect enormous amounts of process data for yield and quality control, including sensor readings, optical inspections of masks and wafers, and electrical test parameters. By applying statistical anomaly detection to this data, one can attempt to catch deviations that might indicate tampering.
Ilhan, Withers, Gietz & Harack (2026), Verifiable Semiconductor Manufacturing (Oxford AIGI) (2026), L714–718
