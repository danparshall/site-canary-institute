P047 Completeness: showing that declarations cover all compute

Can a state show, to a stated confidence, that it has no compute, stockpiles or facilities outside its declarations?

Whether a state can show that its declarations cover essentially all of its compute, leaving no hidden facilities, stockpiles, parallel infrastructure or exit route, and to what confidence.

Status

Analyzed A proposed procedure only: mutual declaration of existing, in-transit and in-production compute, a supply-chain records audit with inspectors counting final chips, comparison against each side's intelligence, then extension to third nations; no confidence level, no pilot, and the plan itself says unknown compute cannot be brought under verification. Facilities clause: Koopmanschap & Barten's desk analysis finds that with active concealment facilities of 10,000 to 100,000 H100-equivalents cannot be ruled out. Stockpile and secret-production clauses: their rough bound of about a million untraced chips. Exit routes and smuggling: stated limits only (Harack et al., Aarne & Petrie).

"Both the US and China should then be able to carry out an audit and inspection of each other’s declaration, and compare it against their private intelligence efforts (to the extent they properly prepared this) to resolve their uncertainties over how much compute each other have."
Dean (2026), Verification Plan (AI 2040 Plan A supplement, AI Futures Project), Phase 2 (Implement the deal) (2026 (no date on page; companion documents dated 2 Jul 2026; read 2026-10-15)), L97
