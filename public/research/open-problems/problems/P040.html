P040 Grid-side detection of small hidden loads

What is the smallest undeclared AI load detectable from grid or utility interval data at a fixed false-alarm rate?

Detecting small hidden AI loads from grid and utility data.

Terms: feeder: a distribution power line serving an area. phase unbalance: unevenness across the three phases of AC power, a tell-tale of some loads. CUSUM: a statistical test that flags a sustained shift in a signal.

Status

Analyzed A desk reference-class analysis of utility electricity records by facility size, using Pilz & Heim's data-centre counts and EIA survey counts: a 10 MW continuous load is visible at the utility level but blends with crypto-mining, continuous-process industrial and campus loads; at 1 MW and 100 kW the paper places the load among flat-load commercial customers "of which the United States has millions", a figure it takes from a count of commercial buildings by floor area (EIA CBECS 2018, Table B6), not by electric load or load factor. No detector built, no interval data analysed and no false-alarm rate, so this survey's false-alarm goal of 0.01 per facility-year is not addressed; assumes utility cooperation; off-grid generation defeats it.

"A 100 kW continuous load fits within the existing population of mid-size commercial customers with refrigeration, telecom, or 24/7 operations, of which the United States has millions (U.S. Energy Information Administration 2022b). There is no externally distinguishing power signature at this scale."
Koopmanschap & Barten (2026), How to Catch a GPU: A Taxonomy of Verification and Enforcement Mechanisms for International AI Agreements (arXiv 2607.22619v1), App. B, Power consumption monitoring (2026 (arXiv 2607.22619v1; stamp reads 16 Jun 2026)), L872–875
