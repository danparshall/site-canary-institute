P046 Detecting and attributing underwater data centres

Can an underwater data centre built for evasion be detected, and told apart from commercial subsea infrastructure (commercial underwater data centres, offshore wind, cables)?

Detecting an underwater data centre built for evasion (heat plume, acoustic and power signatures, cable installations) and telling it apart from commercial subsea infrastructure.

Terms: UDC (underwater data centre): a sealed data centre placed on the seabed and cooled by the surrounding water.

Status

Analyzed A desk analysis (order-of-magnitude physics and logistics) of thermal, acoustic, optical and SAR detection of a 150 to 200 MW, 100,000-H100-equivalent underwater build, finding construction and maintenance the detectable phases; attribution against offshore wind, platforms and ships' generators is unsolved ('detection is not the same as attribution'); no field measurement, no sensor trial.

Thermal detection of an operational pod is unlikely outside shallow, calm water; acoustic detection is marginally more effective, but faces limitations in attribution; and optical/SAR monitoring is most powerful during construction and maintenance
Teague, Rajmohan & Muehlhaeuser (2026), Could Underwater Data Centers Pose a Risk to AI Treaty Verification? (arXiv 2609.18824v1) (2026-09-16), L21–24
