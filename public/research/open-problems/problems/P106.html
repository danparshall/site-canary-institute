P106 On-site physical inspection capacity and practice

How many inspector-days, at what cost and sample size, are needed to verify a given number of accelerators or data centres at a stated detection probability?

Inspector-hours, sample sizes, cost and procedures for physical inspection of chips, racks and data centres.

Terms: N: the number in question, of chips in a hidden cluster, or of chips, racks, accelerators or data centres to be inspected.

Status

Analyzed A design and estimate from 2023: random sampling of chips' logged weight snapshots, with a formula for the samples needed to catch a rule-breaking run at a given probability. For a 10^25 FLOP run among 10^7 chips, caught within 30 days at 90% probability: about 232,000 samples a year, or about 232 inspectors if each checks 1,000 chips a year (an assumption), against 280 IAEA inspectors in 2021. No cost estimate and nothing run; it assumes chips that log weight snapshots, which still do not exist in 2026, and no later paper has updated the numbers. The installed base has roughly reached his assumption (over 15 million H100-equivalents in January 2026, Epoch AI), and 10^25 FLOP is now an ordinary run rather than a frontier one.

This would require ≈ 232 inspectors, slightly smaller than the 280 active IAEA inspectors as of 2021.
Shavit (2023), What Does It Take to Catch a Chinchilla? Verifying Rules on Large-Scale Neural Network Training via Compute Monitoring (2023-05-30), L618–619
