P052 Sampled audit and recomputation rates

What audit and recomputation sampling rate gives a high probability of catching a violation, given that recomputation is slow?

Sampling rates for audit and recomputation.

Terms: π: the fraction of layer blocks the client samples. N: the total number of blocks. k: in Jin et al., the number of blocks an adversary alters; in Amodo's scheme, the number of steps one recomputation takes. p: the chance that a given step is recomputed.

Status

Started For this TEE-based block-commitment scheme, sampling a fraction of layer blocks gives detection probability about 1 − (1 − π)^k against an adversary touching k blocks, implemented in CUDA pipelines and evaluated on Llama-3.1-8B across attack types and block configurations; a source's own system. The slow-recomputation trade-off is only analytic: Amodo's example scheme derives a per-step recompute probability of about 1/k when recomputation takes k steps (p ≈ 0.001 for k = 1000) and notes better strategies may exist. No regime-level audit rate against this survey's detection goal of 90%.

"Even modest verification efforts achieve substantial detection rates. For instance, if an adversary compromises 10% of blocks (k = 100) out of N = 1000 and the client samples only π = 1% of blocks, the detection probability reaches Pdetect ≈ 65%."
Jin et al. (2026), Trusting What You Cannot See: Auditable Fine-Tuning and Inference for Proprietary AI (arXiv 2603.07466v2), section 5.7 (2026-07-22), L871–873
