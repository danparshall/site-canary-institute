P034 Attributing captured traffic to declared workloads

Can analysis of captured traffic attribute it to declared workloads and detect an undeclared workload embedded in a declared one, at stated error rates?

Attributing captured traffic to declared workloads.

Status

Analyzed A protocol design with a security argument: an auditor-owned passive tap hashes inter-node collective payloads into a Merkle tree that is checked against the declared training at challenge time; the authors list the canonical wire-to-tensor mapping ('not a stable public interface today'), auditing that mapping without trusting the trainer's host, and the absence of any NVLink witness as open problems; the paper's prototype concerns the proof system, not the tap or the attribution. Cankaya (2026) separately proposes per-transfer metadata headers (job ID, tensor type, layer, step) so captured samples can be matched to declared workloads, with negligible overhead asserted, not measured. Detecting an undeclared workload embedded in a declared one at stated error rates: no measurement on real traffic; Baker et al. (2025) call the analysis an unsolved problem.

Network anchor. Catches any deviation that changes wire-bound content; deterministic (not statistical) detection on any sampled wire-bound tensor.
Peigne-Lefebvre, Nguyen & Wang (2026), Zero Knowledge Verification for Frontier AI Training Is Possible, arXiv 2606.05433 v2, defense-in-depth (network anchor) (2026-08-22 (arXiv v2)), L1487–1488
