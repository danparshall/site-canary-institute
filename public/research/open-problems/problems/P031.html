P031 Line-rate capture and hashing hardware

Can hardware hash, timestamp and sign every packet at full line rate with zero loss, and at what resource and power cost?

Line-rate capture, hashing and signing hardware.

Terms: FPGA (field-programmable gate array): a chip whose logic can be reconfigured, often used for line-rate packet processing. DPU / SmartNIC: a network card with its own processor that can inspect and process traffic.

Status

Started The authors' own CPU/DPDK benchmark at 400 Gb/s: line-rate hashing for frames of 1500 B and above; at 64 B minimum frames no algorithm reaches line rate within the physical core count; hashing only, with no timestamping or signing, zero loss on real datacenter traffic not shown, and no trusted or simple hardware; not 800G or 1.6T (limited hardware availability); the authors say an FPGA or ASIC is likely needed. Peigne-Lefebvre et al. (2026) still pose formally verified FPGA hashing at 400+ Gbps as open.

For realistic traffic profiles at 1500B and above, AES-128-GMAC and SipHash-1-3-128 are both capable of sustaining line-rate hashing
Reynolds & Foster (2026), Network Traffic Hashing (Amodo Design note), DPDK Summary (2026-07-03), L547
