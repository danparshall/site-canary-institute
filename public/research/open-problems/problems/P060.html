P060 Weight snapshots on accelerators

Can an accelerator take attested, tamper-evident weight/memory snapshots bound to chip state at trigger time, at what overhead and with what storage cost?

Attested, tamper-evident snapshots of weights/memory on chip: support, overhead, timing binding, locating weights, storage.

Terms: HBM (high-bandwidth memory): the fast memory stacked next to an accelerator where model weights and activations are held.

Status

Analyzed Design only: random-time, firmware-raised HBM snapshots hashed on chip and kept in firmware-only non-volatile memory or signed to a verifier-trusted server; overhead and storage cost discussed qualitatively (synchronised chips hang during a snapshot; terabyte-scale checkpoints). Assumes hardware-security-module features that Harack et al. call 'speculative' and 'not yet available'. Harack Appendix L.4 adds a design sketch for a timed weight-residency challenge of about 50 ms, 'an area for future work'. Nothing built. Related built work: Monfared et al. 2026 measured a gap above 350 ms between HBM-resident and host-RAM-resident 60 GB challenges on an H100, a residency probe rather than an attested snapshot, so a cross-reference.

A chip’s weight-shards can be captured by snapshotting the chip’s high-bandwidth memory, which contains the weights. These snapshots must be saved at random times, as otherwise the Prover can swap out the true weights for a fake set of weights right before the scheduled logging.
Shavit (2023), What Does It Take to Catch a Chinchilla? Verifying Rules on Large-Scale Neural Network Training via Compute Monitoring (arXiv 2303.11341v2) (2023-05-30), L607–609
