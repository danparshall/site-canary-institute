P067 Chip operating licences and off-switches: the on-chip mechanism

Can a signed, time-limited operating licence or remote off-switch be built into accelerators so that it resists downgrade, bypass and stockpiling?

Whether a signed, time-limited operating licence or remote off-switch can be built on shipping accelerators, its renewal and anti-stockpiling design, and its resistance to firmware downgrade, bypass and physical key extraction.

Status

Analyzed Designs only, nothing built: Petrie's on-die design of about 10,000 redundant licence-checking security blocks with an attack-and-countermeasure table (logic flaws, licence reuse, glitching, focused-ion-beam edits, key extraction); RAND's offline licensing with metered compute budgets (Kulp et al. 2024); CNAS's timed licences as the only robust remote disable (Aarne, Fist & Withers 2024). No shipping accelerator enforces a signed time-limited licence (Harack et al. 2025: 'not yet available'). Downgrade and bypass resistance: asserted, not tested; no red-team of licensing firmware on an AI GPU. Stockpiling: discussed as a risk (cycle-counter banking, powered-off reserve chips) with design responses, nothing shown. Related but not counted as a tested failure of this family: CNAS reports that Nvidia's driver-level hash-rate limiter on consumer GPUs was fully circumvented about a year after release, a different mechanism family reported second-hand.

In this paper, we propose a significantly more secure design that would embed thousands of hardware security blocks into upcoming generations of AI chips. Each security block would individually check for up-to-date usage authorization, and block essential chip operations if this approval is not received.
Petrie (2025), Embedded Off-Switches for AI Compute, arXiv 2509.07637 (2025-09-09 (arXiv v1)), L35–37
