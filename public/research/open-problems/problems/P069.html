P069 Hardware enforcement of permitted use

Can chips be restricted to approved code, inference-only, or bounded training, and what throughput penalty does the best circumvention attack achieve?

Restricting chips to approved code, inference-only operation or bounded training, the cost of circumventing such restrictions, and the time to convert deployed compute to verified inference-only operation.

Status

Analyzed Designs only: RAND's licence-metered throttle (bounded training by operation budget, with a hardware cutoff sketch) and CNAS's on-chip restriction to approved code or models; Scher & Thiergart list inference-only chips and an operation disallow-list as building blocks and call them possibly 'extremely difficult or effectively impossible'. No prototype has refused a non-compliant job in a test (Harack et al. 2025). The circumvention clause has no work: no measured throughput penalty for the best attack, and RAND itself notes a clock-slowing throttle 'might be easy to circumvent with a cheap FIB edit'. Stated limit, not a failure: software-only mechanisms can verify use but not enforce it (Petrie & Aarne 2025).

When the counter for a functional unit (such as a floating-point multiplier block) exceeds the threshold set by the license, a multiplexer placed before the output of the functional unit can select an alternative output value of zero, ignoring the actual computation results.
Kulp et al. (2024), Hardware-Enabled Governance Mechanisms (RAND WR-A3056-1) (2024-01), L2186–2189
