P089 Zero-knowledge proofs of training and inference

What is the largest model for which a zero-knowledge proof of training or inference is practical, and at what prover overhead?

Zero-knowledge proofs of training and inference.

Terms: zero-knowledge proof: a cryptographic proof that a statement is true (e.g. that a computation was done correctly) while revealing nothing else about the inputs.

Status

Started Inference only: a built, open-source ZK proof system measured on LLaMa-2-13B and OPT-13B (one A100 plus 12 CPU cores): 803 s to prove a 2048-token inference, proof under 200 kB, verification in seconds; impractical at production request rates, and later systems (ZKTorch, zkGPT) remain at GPT-2 to Llama-2-7B scale with minutes per token. Training: no ZK proof of full-LLM backpropagation in native floating point has been shown (Peigné-Lefebvre et al. 2026); their 36-month, single-digit-percent-overhead estimate is a paper estimate, and zkLLM's authors write that ZK proofs of LLM training may pose insurmountable challenges.

"for LLMs boasting 13 billion parameters, our approach enables the generation of a correctness proof for the entire inference process in under 15 minutes."
Sun, Li & Zhang (2024), zkLLM: Zero Knowledge Proofs for Large Language Models (ACM CCS 2024), Abstract (2024-04-24), L84–86
