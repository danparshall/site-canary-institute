P078 Compute accounting: MFU, HFU and declared operations

Can a model predict achievable hardware utilisation closely enough to detect padded or undeclared-efficiency compute, at a tolerable false-alarm rate?

MFU/HFU modelling and detecting padded or undeclared-efficiency compute.

Terms: MFU / HFU (model-FLOP utilisation / hardware-FLOP utilisation): the fraction of a chip's peak compute a training run actually uses - two accounting conventions that differ in how they count recomputed work.

Status

Analyzed A proposed method with a desk analysis of its error sources: estimate achievable utilisation for a declared workload and cluster, compare with declared operations, and back it with physical signatures when an operator's efficiency exceeds the estimate. No model has been validated against measured runs, no padding or no-op detector has been built, and the false-alarm rate for honest operators is only flagged as a risk ('honest Provers [may be] unable to account for their estimated compute use'). Attacks named without defences shown: secret backend optimisation, speculative decoding (1.5-3x), Pareto-inefficient menu choices, restructuring to minimise logged operations. Cankaya's system overview proposes a resource-occupation simulation cross-compared with throughput, also paper-only.

For a given workload type and cluster, optimal MFU could be coarsely approximated as the MFU known to have been achieved on similar workloads and clusters, on which there is private and public data (Epoch AI, 2024b). Optimal MFU could be more precisely estimated with theoretical modeling (Erdil and Schneider-Joseph, 2024; Erdil, 2025). Still, significant error bars may remain.
Baker, Kulp, Marks, Brundage & Heim (2025), Verifying International Agreements on AI: Six Layers of Verification (RAND WR-A4077-1) (2025-07-25 (v2)), L3040–3045
