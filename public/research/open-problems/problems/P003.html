P003 Enclosure PUFs

Can an enclosure-scale hardware fingerprint (PUF) hold a stable key across years of thermal cycling and vibration while resisting probing attacks?

Enclosure-scale PUFs: stability, key generation, probing attacks.

Terms: PUF (physically unclonable function): a unique, hard-to-copy hardware fingerprint from manufacturing variations; here measured across a whole enclosure to generate a key.

Status

Started Batteryless flexPCB mesh cover deriving a PUF key for a multi-chip PCB embedded system, aiming at FIPS 140-2 Level 3: statistics over 115 covers, physical attacks, and a -20 to +60 C chamber test on one board with three covers plus accelerated ageing of foils. Not accelerator scale or 700–1,200 W, no vibration test, and multi-year key stability not shown (the authors suggest re-enrolling in the field), so the headline's years-of-cycling clause is open; probing resistance is argued by design (keys not reachable without destroying the PUF) rather than tested against microprobing. Kulp et al. (2024) report the demonstration second-hand; the stability question is posed as open by Harack et al. (2025) and the RAND PUF-enclosures RFD.

We demonstrate the feasibility of our concept … Practical results including statistics over a set of 115 flexPCB covers, physical attacks, and environmental testing support our design rationale.
Immler, Obermaier, Ng, Ke, Lee, Lim, Oh, Wee & Sigl (2019), Secure Physical Enclosures from Covers with Tamper-Resistance (IACR TCHES) (2019), L30–33
