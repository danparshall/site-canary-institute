P119 Legal limits on verification operations

What legal limits apply to verification operations: retaining captured data across borders, defensive jamming, and non-interference with national technical means?

The legal limits on verification operations: retaining captured data across borders, defensive jamming, sensors in territorial waters, and non-interference with national technical means.

Status

Started One leg only, non-interference with national technical means: a count from the Alva Myrdal Centre treaty database (99 mechanisms, 33 treaties, 1959 to 2015) showing such clauses exist only in bilateral treaties, so a multilateral AI treaty's clause would be novel; Scher (2026) notes SALT I to New START authorise NTM and prohibit interference (precedent). Cross-border retention of captured data (Cankaya: 'will vary on a case-by-case basis') and defensive jamming (Cankaya: 'out-of-scope') have no work. Teague et al.'s estimate that passive acoustic sensors would need to sit inside the 12-nautical-mile territorial sea is for the statement's territorial-waters clause, not the headline (related).

All 10 NTM non-interference clauses and all 7 concealment prohibitions in the dataset are bilateral.
