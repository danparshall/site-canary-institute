P021 Trustworthiness and leakage of monitoring sensors and data

Can facility sensors be built that both parties trust — verifier trusts the readings, prover trusts they do not exfiltrate — with a quantified leak bound?

Whether facility sensors and their data can be trusted and leak little.

Status

Started Leakage of verifier-facing releases derived from real physical measurements (H100 and B200 node power traces, RAPL power traces, GPU electromagnetic traces, measured workload profiles) over six verification tasks, measured as conditional mutual information: this covers the prover's quantified leak bound for what the verifier receives. The verifier-trusts-the-readings clause is out of scope ('MID does not make a compromised evidence source honest'); exfiltration by the sensor hardware itself (bits per second per channel) and a mutually trusted sensor: nothing built; Harack's prover-side mitigations are paper only.

Across these tasks, MID produces three releases with perfect held-out verification and zero measured collateral leakage, while the remaining tasks yield explicit privacy–utility frontiers.
Abdelghafar & Kulp (2026), Privacy-Preserving AI Verification via Minimal Information Disclosure (arXiv 2608.02774v1) (2026-08-03), L22–24
