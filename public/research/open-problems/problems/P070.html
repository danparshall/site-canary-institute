P070 Compute metering and trusted time

Can an on-chip compute and activity meter and a trusted timer count accurately and resist clock, fault and counter-manipulation attacks?

On-chip compute meters, counters and trusted time.

Status

Analyzed Design only: a meter built on existing performance counters with licence-set caps, plus proposed protections (monotonic counters, lockstep redundancy, shielding against fault injection, sanity checks between counters). Nothing fabricated or measured; Ansari 2026 records 'the absence of functional prototypes', and current counters are 'accessible via firmware interfaces and were designed for debugging rather than tamper-resistant governance'. Trusted-time clause: design-level only; CNAS proposes counting clock cycles instead of time because tamper-proof time 'generally is considered very difficult', and RAND says there is 'no airtight defense' against clock attacks. Fault-injection resistance and sanity-check false-alarm rates: no work.

Since these values are already collected in hardware, additional logic could be added to set a maximum value for each counter, determined by a license.
Kulp et al. (2024), Hardware-Enabled Governance Mechanisms (RAND WR-A3056-1) (2024-01), L2179–2180
