P049 Bounding unmonitored and undeclared compute

Given stock accounting of accelerators, what is the tightest bound on undeclared compute, and what is the smallest undeclared training run the regime can detect?

Stock accounting of accelerators and the resulting bound on undeclared compute and on what the regime can detect.

Status

Analyzed A desk estimate from published production and ownership-tracing figures: roughly a million chips untraced, so an approximate upper bound on the largest hidden cluster; the authors call the exact lower bound hard to estimate from public information. No regime stock audit has been run. The detectable-run clause has only models: Shavit's chip-sampling analysis (unmonitored compute sets an implicit lower bound on detectable run size), Dean's AI 2040 model estimate of about 0.5% of world compute divertible, and the same paper's 10 MW (about 10,000 H100-equivalent) satellite threshold, all paper-only. This survey's run size of 10^24 FLOP is not addressed.

"As noted earlier, 15.7 million of the 20.2 million chips produced can be traced to the owning company. Much of the remaining 4.5 million can likely also be traced, leaving on the order of a million chips unaccounted for. This yields an approximate bound on the size of the largest hidden cluster."
Koopmanschap & Barten (2026), How to Catch a GPU: A Taxonomy of Verification and Enforcement Mechanisms for International AI Agreements (arXiv 2607.22619v1), section 5.3.2 (2026), L491–494
