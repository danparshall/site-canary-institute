P082 Statistical inference verification (TOPLOC / DiFR family)

Do statistical inference-verification methods reliably catch cheating with few false failures on large models, and do adaptive attacks defeat them?

Statistical inference verification (TOPLOC / Token-DiFR family).

Terms: TOPLOC, Token-DiFR: named published methods that statistically check whether a model really produced the outputs it claims, without re-running the whole computation. k: in top-k sampling, the number of most likely tokens the sampler chooses among; the "simulated k = 2 bug" makes 1% of tokens a uniform pick from the top two.

Status

Started Token-DiFR re-implemented by a third party and run on a tray of H100s against non-adaptive attacks (substituted models including gpt-oss-120b versus 20b, LoRA append and append-on-trigger on Qwen2.5-7B); the best honest-versus-cheating pass-rate gap was about 25%, the threshold must be set per model, and most misses were outputs under 5 tokens. Adaptive-attacks clause: the DiFR paper (Karvonen et al. 2025) shows Token-DiFR robust to one simple adaptive attack (temperature tuned to match mean cross-entropy, which defeats cross-entropy checks); stronger adaptive attacks untested, and Amodo's own note says its testing 'only covers a fraction of possible attacks'. Models tested are well below frontier size; the smallest deviations on Qwen3-30B-A3B 'remain difficult to separate' in the DiFR paper.

Our results add further evidence that Token DiFR can identify different incorrect-model attacks reliably across a small range of prompts.
Amodo Design (2026), An Inference Verification Prototype, Stage 1 (design note) (2026-06-29), L235
