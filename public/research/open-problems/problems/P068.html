P068 Licence issuance and key governance

Who should issue operating licences and hold the signing keys, and how can issuance and emergency-disable powers be made abuse-resistant?

Who issues operating licences and holds the signing keys (criteria, k-of-n signer sets across jurisdictions, bootstrapping, liveness), and how issuance and emergency-disable powers are kept abuse-resistant.

Terms: k of n (k-of-n): a threshold scheme in which an action needs the approval of any k of the n parties, so it keeps working after up to n−k keys are lost.

Status

Analyzed A protocol sketch with pseudocode: k-of-n multilateral signing of licence and guarantee updates, graded quorums by update type, and signer-set changes by update. No prototype (Ansari 2026: 'No prototype exists'), and the open parts are stated by the proposers themselves: who the licence authority is, how the signer set is bootstrapped, what k and n should be, timeout handling for unresponsive signatories, proofs of non-issuance ('would probably require hardware-backed evidence from all devices that contain the private key'), and the blast radius of a stolen signing key. Abuse resistance of emergency disable: Baker et al. 2025 judge it not to need significant R&D because proposals exist; that is a readiness opinion, not evidence.

The update process could require that the update (or lack of update) be multilaterally approved by k of n parties, so that no single actor could unilaterally impose excessive restrictions (as discussed for an international context in Part III). The pseudocode in algorithm 1 shows how multi-party update approval could be implemented.
Petrie & Aarne (2025), Technical Options for Flexible Hardware-Enabled Guarantees (flexHEG Part 2) (2025-04 (April 2025 report; arXiv v1 2025-06-03)), L1636–1639
