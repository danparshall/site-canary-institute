P048 Monitoring data sources and intelligence

Can non-technical data sources (payment and customer-identity records, financial intelligence, cloud metadata) identify large AI-chip rentals or purchases, and how reliably?

Non-technical monitoring data sources and intelligence.

Terms: KYC (know-your-customer): the customer-identity records firms must collect; used here as a non-technical signal of who is renting or buying large amounts of AI compute.

Status

Analyzed Design and feasibility argument: compute providers as KYC-style intermediaries using billing records and non-confidential metadata to classify large training and inference workloads and account for compute; no recall or precision for identifying chip rentals is measured, and detailed workload properties are said to need confidential computing. Ansari notes metadata collection stops at individual providers (no cross-provider or on-premises coverage); Leklem & Maheshwari propose a monitoring network fed by cloud API patterns and export data; Scher's 'might also use financial intelligence' is a one-line suggestion. Payment-record and financial-intelligence methods have no analysis.

Several of these techniques could be used in real-time to establish whether a customer is engaging in an activity that might be subject to regulatory oversight. If compute providers were to implement these techniques, they could likely distinguish whether their customers are engaging in activities such as training a large model, or engaging in inference at scale, as well as quantify the amount of compute consumed by large workloads
Heim et al. (2024), Governing Through the Cloud: The Intermediary Role of Compute Providers in AI Regulation (GovAI) (2024-03-26 (arXiv v2)), L683–687
