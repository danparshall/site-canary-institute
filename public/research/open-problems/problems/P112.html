P112 Replay mismatches and dispute settlement

What is the baseline rate of benign replay mismatches, and can a dispute procedure attribute a mismatch to hardware fault versus cheating?

The rate of benign mismatches in replay checks, and whether a dispute procedure can tell a hardware fault from cheating.

Status

Started Delegation setting: when two executions of the same ML program disagree, a referee bisects by checkpoint hashes to one training step and one operator, re-executes it and rules; benign floating-point mismatches are removed by bitwise-reproducible operators, implemented and benchmarked on DistilBERT and Llama-3.1 1B and 8B in FP32. It finds which party is wrong, not whether the cause was a hardware fault or cheating; no baseline rate of benign replay mismatches is measured; Cankaya (2026) claims his own bitwise replay settles statistical false alarms, a self-report at the same stage; GPU-stack training replay at frontier scale not shown.

we design Verde, a dispute arbitration protocol that efficiently handles the large scale and graph-based computational model of modern ML programs. For (2), we build RepOps (Reproducible Operators), a library that eliminates hardware “nondeterminism” by controlling the order of floating point operations performed on all hardware. Our implementation shows that refereed delegation achieves both strong guarantees for clients and practical overheads for compute providers.
Arun et al. (2025), Verde: Verification via Refereed Delegation for Machine Learning Programs, arXiv 2502.19405 (2025-02-26), L47–53
