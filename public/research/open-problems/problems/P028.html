P028 Taps on scale-up and electrical interconnects (NVLink)

Can traffic on high-speed electrical links between accelerators (such as NVLink/NVSwitch) and other non-optical interconnects be captured or witnessed at full line rate by a trustworthy add-on device?

Capturing or witnessing NVLink/NVSwitch, copper and other non-optical interconnect traffic at line rate.

Terms: NVLink / NVSwitch: Nvidia's high-speed electrical links and switch chips that connect GPUs within a server or rack.

Status

Analyzed Desk analysis of two designs for copper (NVLink-class) links, a modified switch ASIC that copies sampled traffic from its digital fabric and a bump-in-wire retimer exposing its equalized bitstream, with the author's expectations of about 60 ns added per hop, dependence on the switch or retimer vendor, and at least a year of lead time; nothing built or measured. Milton & Reynolds (Amodo, 2026) likewise describe an optical-electrical-optical retimer tap and an electrical interposer at the transceiver port as designs 'not commercially available' that need to be designed and tested. Full line rate and the trustworthiness of an active device: argued (active electronics add attack surface), not tested. Bidirectional circulator-optics TPU pod links: no work. Peigne-Lefebvre et al. (2026) state NVLink traffic has no physical witness.

Bump-in-wire retimer tap: Insert a signal retimer into the copper link and extract a digital copy from its internal equalized bitstream.
Cankaya (2026), Research Note: The Fundamentals and Feasibility of Secure Network Taps for Verifying AI Datacenter Use (Substack), section 3.4 Copper vs. optical (2026-04-30 (live page read 2026-10-21)), L150
