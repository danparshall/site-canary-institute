P013 Designer backdoors and hidden functionality

Can a verifier gain assurance that a chip design is free of designer-inserted backdoors or hidden execution paths, at a stated detection rate on seeded trojans?

Detecting designer backdoors and hidden functionality.

Status

Analyzed Design options only, for the guarantee processor: open-sourcing the design and logic for third-party analysis against physical scans of randomly selected chips, executing guarantee logic on publicly auditable FPGAs, and running redundant guarantee processors from different manufacturers in lock-step; flexHEG Part 3 adds collaborative design with justification of every choice. Nothing built, no outside review of real silicon, and no detection rate on seeded trojans anywhere in the held sources; the binning clause (dormant functionality) has only RAND's design rule that there be none.

To increase trust that backdoors have not been added to the Guarantee Processor design or manufacturing process, some options are: … Open-sourcing the guarantee processor design and guarantee logic, which could then be analyzed and compared with physical scans of randomly selected chips.
Petrie & Aarne (2025), Technical Options for Flexible Hardware-Enabled Guarantees (flexHEG Part 2) (2025-04), L606–617
