P062 Integrity and attack surface of the verification apparatus

Can the verifier-side infrastructure itself (recomputation servers, taps, sensors) be trusted and resist a state attacker, and how much attack surface does it add?

Whether verifier-side infrastructure (recomputation servers, taps, sensors, the regime as a whole) can itself be trusted and resist state attackers.

Status

Analyzed Design only, from a self-deprecated working draft (June 2026, since rewritten; treat as dated): a verifier-side auditing environment built from three independently sourced trusted computing bases whose outputs are compared before leaving an air-gap, plus an instructor-executor split with formally verified logic for the simple parts. Nothing built; no estimate of added attack surface. Recomputation-server hardening has no work (AI 2040: 'needs significant research'; Amodo: 'has not been explored'). Comer et al. 2026 (RAND) design a secure inference facility against nation-state attackers, but prover-side, so a cross-reference.

At least three distinct TCBs (different supply chains, hardware and software. One trusted by the prover, the verifier and a third-party) make up three small clusters, all inside the prover’s datacenter and inside their own air-gap.
Cankaya (2026), A System Overview for Near-Term, Low-Trust AI Compute Verification, version 0.2 working draft (LessWrong, archived revision 1.4.0) (2026-06-24), L481

For P062.2: Does current GPU confidential computing / attestation (e.g. SPDM sessions) use post-quantum key exchange, closing harvest-now-decrypt-later?

  • Towards Quantum-Resistant Trusted Computing: Architectures for Post-Quantum Integrity Verification Techniques — Grazia D'Onghia, Antonio Lioy, arXiv, 2026Firmware, being the first layer of trusted software, faces unique risks due to its longevity and difficult update. The transition of firmware protection to Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC) is urgent, since it reduces the risk derived from exposing all computing and network devices to quantum-based attacks.
  • Towards Stateless Post-Quantum Remote Attestation for IoT Using TPM and DICE — Michael Eckel, Janik Gorbracht, Georgios Gkoktsis et al., IEEE International Conference on Trust, Security and Privacy in Computing and Communications (TrustCom), 2025This paper presents a powerful stateless post-quantum remote attestation approach for IoT devices, leveraging authenticated and encrypted (AEAD) challenges within TPM and DICE-based attestation.
  • github.comCaliptra refuses to boot firmware unless the firmware was dual-signed by the key reported by SoC ROM's fuse registers.
  • www.dmtf.orgTo cope with the potential threats of emergent quantum computers, the SPDM supports both traditional and post-quantum cryptography (PQC) algorithms for the signature and authenticated key exchange protocols.
