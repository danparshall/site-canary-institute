P005 Facility air-gap and enclosure containment

Has an air-gapped facility or pod been red-teamed against electromagnetic, acoustic, optical and personnel channels, with a verified limit on what can leak out?

Air-gap and shielded-enclosure containment of facilities and pods.

Terms: air-gap: physically isolating a facility from outside networks. dB attenuation: how much a shield cuts a signal, in decibels (every 10 dB is a tenfold reduction). SCIF: a shielded, access-controlled secure room.

Status

Analyzed Desk study only: a literature survey of physical side channels and an example defence architecture for rack or pod enclosures, with an estimated bill of materials below 1% of cluster cost while tolerating single-digit kbit/s of covert bandwidth; the author calls it a two-week sprint, 'far from conclusive'. Nothing built; no facility or pod red-teamed; no verified leakage limit; the RF attenuation of cheaply welded sheet-metal enclosures is an assumption, not a measurement. Single-computer exfiltration demonstrations held in the collection (Fansmitter acoustic exfiltration at 15 bits/min, Deep-TEMPEST HDMI, Synesthesia screen acoustics) are attack measurements at PC scale, not containment tests, and are cross-references because the headline names facility or pod scale. The Proofworks workshop 'seeming consensus' that 1-bit air-gapping is feasible is an opinion.

I present an example of a defense architecture towards the end of this post. It leverages a synergy of cheap enclosures, sensors, jamming, unannounced hardware inspections and physical security
