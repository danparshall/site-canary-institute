P037 Encrypted traffic and taps

Can a tap verify encrypted inter-accelerator traffic through confined key disclosure, and at what security trade-off and throughput cost?

Taps versus encrypted interconnect traffic.

Terms: encrypted interconnect traffic (TEE/CC): data sent between accelerators encrypted by a trusted execution environment or confidential-computing feature. confined key disclosure: giving a tap just enough key material to check traffic without exposing everything.

Status

Analyzed Desk analysis of key disclosure for tapped encrypted traffic: where encryption runs outside a trusted execution environment the prover can retain session keys and share them after sessions expire; for TEE-encrypted fabrics post-hoc decryption is called fundamentally difficult and an active man-in-the-middle tap is sketched as a possible workaround with unestimated overhead; the security trade-off is argued (taps as a substitute for TEE isolation against a malicious operator), not tested. Throughput cost of any such tap: no work. Security Level 5 encrypted-traffic cruxes were found at a workshop and not resolved.

This means that for non-TEE encrypted fabrics, the operator (i.e., the prover) can retain keys to enable post-hoc decryption of tapped ciphertext. Encryption alone does not foreclose verification.
Cankaya (2026), Research Note: The Fundamentals and Feasibility of Secure Network Taps for Verifying AI Datacenter Use (Substack), appendix D encrypted traffic (2026-04-30 (live page read 2026-10-21)), L348
