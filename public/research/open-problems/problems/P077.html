P077 Cross-hardware bit-exact replay and emulation

Can a computation be replayed or emulated bit-exactly on hardware other than the prover's (other SKUs, vendors, CPU), and at what slowdown?

Replaying or emulating computations bit-exactly on hardware other than the prover's (other SKUs, vendors, CPU emulation, verification-chip variants).

Terms: GEMM (general matrix multiply): the dense matrix-multiplication that dominates AI compute. MoE (mixture of experts): a model design that routes each input through only some of its sub-networks.

Status

Started Software-only CPU emulation of NVIDIA GPU inference arithmetic (L40, L40S, A100, H100 profiles), dense transformer blocks of Qwen3-4B plus one full forward pass; bit-exact against CUTLASS, and against cuBLAS through a per-SKU dispatch catalogue except nvjet kernels on H100 at short sequences. Not mixture-of-experts, not AMD, TPU or Ascend, not training. Slowdown clause: not measured; the appendix estimates CPU need and says the figures 'likely need to be multiplied by at least 10x' for reduction-tree emulation, so that clause is paper-only. Cross-hardware next-token agreement without emulation is about 85% one GPU generation apart (Hausenloy & Li 2026).

The emulator matches CUTLASS bit-exactly at every tested hardware/sequence-length combination.
Cankaya (2026), Bit-Exact AI Inference Verification Without Performance Tradeoffs, arXiv 2606.00279v2 (2026-06-05 (v2)), L757–758
