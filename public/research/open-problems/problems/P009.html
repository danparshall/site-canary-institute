P009 Production oversight of chips and mechanism components

What institutional oversight - monitoring, inspection standards, independent testing and verified destruction - covers the production of chips and of the components that verification mechanisms depend on?

Institutional oversight of chip and mechanism-component production: monitoring regimes, inspection standards and capacity, independent testing, verified destruction of failed units, tool-use controls, and retroactively establishing trust in already-produced chips.

Terms: p: the fraction of devices sampled for imaging. d: the chance that imaging catches a compromised device it inspects. n: the number of compromised devices.

Status

Analyzed A design for an international body's oversight of guarantee-processor production: manufacturing equipment locked to the approved design, independent testing by a party other than the manufacturer, continuous camera and on-site staff oversight, tamper-evident shipping, extensive testing and imaging of a 1/1000 sample (the authors' own arithmetic gives a 63% chance of catching a 1,000-device compromised batch), verifiable destruction of failed devices, published processes and authorised penetration tests. Nothing instituted or piloted. Oversight of accelerator fabs themselves is only an analogy to nuclear-fuel monitoring (Shavit 2023); retroactive trust in already-produced chips is a proposal to sample and destructively reverse-engineer existing components (flexHEG Part 1). No held document reports any oversight regime in operation.

Each guarantee processor should be subjected to automated, independent testing. … the testing should be done by a different party than the one that manufactured it, ideally either by the international body, several member states, or both.
Aarne & Petrie (2025), International Security Applications of Flexible Hardware-Enabled Guarantees (flexHEG Part 3) (2025-04), L436–441
