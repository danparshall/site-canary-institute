P097 Detecting distributed training from network traffic

Can a classifier on network-traffic metadata detect low-communication distributed training at high true-positive and low false-positive rates, and does it survive deliberate traffic shaping?

Detecting distributed training from network traffic.

Status

Analyzed A desk analysis of the headline's question: detection must use volume and routing; DiLoCo-style training at under 40 Mbps sits below average household upload speed; jitter, variable-rate streaming, pauses and VPN routing can disguise it, so the author expects the requirements to favour evaders. The 40 Mbps figure comes from the paper's simulation, not a measured run. No classifier designed, trained or tested on traffic; no detection or false-positive rate for this survey's goals. Moon et al.'s large-data-transfer threshold is a proposal for detecting training through cloud ingress, a neighbouring question. Koopmanschap & Barten's note that concealment removes the signature is an argument.

any efforts at detecting distributed training by monitoring internet traffic would have to rely on analysis of the volume and routing, not on the content … the traffic can be disguised into almost any irregular pattern. Traffic shaping methods include jitter, variable-rate streaming, and intermittent pauses.
Rahman (2026), Does Distributed Training Undermine Compute Governance? (MIRI; arXiv 2605.29359) (2026-05-28), L1144–1153
