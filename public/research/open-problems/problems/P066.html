P066 Guarantee processor and verification co-hardware

Can a guarantee processor (verification co-hardware) be interposed on accelerator I/O at acceptable overhead, with binding that detects its removal?

Guarantee processors and verification co-hardware: overhead, binding, feature set.

Terms: guarantee processor (verification co-hardware): a separate, trusted chip placed alongside an accelerator to watch its inputs and outputs and enforce or attest rules.

Status

Analyzed A design, not a build: the flexHEG Guarantee Processor plus Interlock sitting on the accelerator data path, with four candidate placements and a qualitative latency/throughput trade-off (requirements rise the closer the interlock is to the die). Overhead clause: not analysed anywhere; Ansari 2026 says the performance cost of mediating all chip I/O is 'not yet addressed in the published literature'. Binding clause: only qualitative statements (retrofitted hardware 'may be easier for an attacker to decouple'; flexHEG Part 3 poses how the 'brain' could be integrated so it cannot be easily swapped out as an open question); no pairing scheme, no detection rate. The flexHEG team's power-measurement prototype is a retrofit guarantee processor that makes external measurements and does not sit on the I/O path, so it is a cross-reference, not evidence for this headline.

The Interlock could be implemented in several ways, including 1) as an IP block within the accelerator die, 2) as a chiplet sitting near the accelerator die, 3) as a part of high bandwidth memory (HBM), or 4) as a separate component that operates as a network relay or switch.
Petrie & Aarne (2025), Technical Options for Flexible Hardware-Enabled Guarantees (flexHEG Part 2) (2025-04 (April 2025 report; arXiv v1 2025-06-03)), L1054–1056
