P054 Inspection-game modelling of the regime

What do inspection-game models say the regime's optimal inspection rate and detectable-cheat parameters are, and how do they shift as thresholds fall over time?

Inspection-game models of the regime and their parameters.

Terms: inspection game: a mathematical model of an inspector versus a party that may cheat. In Avenhaus & Canty's notation, d is the inspected party's incentive to cheat and b the cost to it of being caught; the d/b (gain-to-sanction) ratio compares the two.

Status

Analyzed A game-theoretic model of a cloud provider detecting threshold evasion, with no empirical data: it locates a detection gap at a fixed FLOP band under FLOP-only reporting and says more models will fill it as software improves; it derives no inspection rate, no gain-to-sanction ratio and no treaty-regime equilibrium. Shavit (2023) section 3.2 gives a closed-form samples-per-year model (a sampling model, not a game). The inspection-game literature held in the collection (Avenhaus and co-authors) models nuclear safeguards, not this regime. The 'thresholds fall over time' clause has no model.

"We analyzed a simple model of evasion and showed that a detection gap exists in the model and contains AI trained on biological sequence data."
