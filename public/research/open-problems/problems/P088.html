P088 Proof-of-learning / proof-of-training-transcript

Is there a proof-of-learning/transcript scheme with a formal soundness bound against an adaptive spoofer, and at what verifier cost as a fraction of training?

Proof-of-learning / proof-of-training-transcript soundness and cost.

Terms: proof-of-learning (PoL) / proof-of-training-transcript (PoTT): schemes that produce evidence a specific training run actually happened as described, checkable by a verifier.

Status

Started Heuristic Proof-of-Training-Data defences (certified-randomness seed, local memorization tests) tested on GPT-2-124M and Pythia checkpoints against all attacks then published in the Proof-of-Learning literature, at about 1.3% of training compute for the verifier; language models only, single attacks only, composite and adaptive attacks and online RL untested, and the Verifier sees raw data and weights. No formal soundness bound exists: the earlier Proof-of-Learning family (Jia et al. 2021) was broken by Fang et al. (2023), a tested failure of that mechanism family; Shavit (2023) gives the formal PoTT definition and leaves provably secure methods to future work.

"Our methods can be run cheaply, adding as little as 1.3% of the original training run’s compute. … The verification strategies we describe are not provably robust, but are intended as an opening proposal"
Choi, Shavit & Duvenaud (2023), Tools for Verifying Neural Models' Training Data (Proof-of-Training-Data), sections 1 and 4 (2023-07-02), L71–76
