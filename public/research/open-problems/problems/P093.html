P093 Workload-type classification from power and telemetry

How accurately can classifiers separate training from inference from non-AI work using telemetry or power, and does that accuracy survive an adaptive evader and hardware drift?

Accuracy, evasion robustness and generalisation of classifiers that tell training from inference from non-AI work using on-board telemetry or off-chip/rack/facility power.

Terms: β (beta): the largest hidden computation a power trace cannot rule out, as a fraction of the declared machine capacity; β (beta) is its worst case over declared utilisation (how busy the declared work keeps the chip).

Status

Started External power draw of a single NVIDIA H200 NVL sampled at 10 MHz, 5 s traces, 17 LLM families and 25 non-AI workloads, authors' own classifier; four evasion strategies tested by the authors, who say this is not a comprehensive adversarial evaluation: three caught at or above 99%, diluted LoRA at 48-88% without the rescue rule, below this survey's detection goal of 90%; cross-device and cross-generation generalisation 'remains to be established'; single node. Corroborating own-method report: Rahman & Tajdari (2026) classify training against non-training from zero-overhead GPU telemetry at 98.2% across 9 GPU types, single node up to 8 GPUs, with dilution and idle-padding evasion tested. Harack et al. (2025) 'not yet possible to reliably know' predates both.

"we separate training from inference and from non-AI computation with an accuracy of 97% and a macro-averaged F1 score of 0.955, evaluated on model families unseen during training" … "The fourth, diluted low-rank adaptation (LoRA), is detected 48–88% of the time with a hardened classifier"
Gargiulo & Kulp (2026), Workload Identification with Physical Side Channels for AI Governance, arXiv 2609.00309, abstract (2026-08-31), L24–32
