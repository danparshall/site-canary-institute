P058 GPU TEE security evidence

What independent, non-vendor security evidence exists for GPU secure enclaves (TEEs) against physical and side-channel attack, and how many in-scope breaks have been found?

Independent security evidence for GPU TEEs, incl. physical and side-channel attacks.

Terms: TEE (trusted execution environment): an isolated, hardware-protected area of a processor whose code and data the machine's owner cannot read or alter.

Status

Started One independent test of NVIDIA GPU confidential computing under physical attack, and it found a break. Academic researchers built a DDR5 memory-bus probe for under $1,000, extracted Intel's attestation key from a fully trusted Xeon in about 15 minutes, and ran a workload on an RTX 3060 outside any secure enclave while passing H100 confidential-computing attestation relayed from a rented H100, because the GPU attestation is not tied to a specific VM. The break goes through the CPU; the H100's own memory and enclave were not attacked, and both vendors call physical attacks out of scope. An independent reverse-engineering of H100 confidential computing (Gu et al. 2025) finds the boot chain and attestation sound within NVIDIA's threat model, but host-visible plaintext command metadata that allows partial reordering or dropping of firmware calls, unprotected command queues, a transfer-size timing channel and still-readable registers: leaks and partial integrity loss, not a break. No physical attack on a data-centre GPU's HBM is held. A Rowhammer attack on workstation GPUs (Lin et al. 2026, GPUBreach) is a cross-reference: its authors list confidential-computing mode as untested.

proves the CPU interfaces directly with a genuine CC GPU. However, the attestation does not identify what the GPU is running.
