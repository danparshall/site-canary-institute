P004 Package- and board-level tamper detection

Can non-destructive inspection detect package- or board-level tampering - circuit edits, re-encapsulation, added components - at a stated detection and false-alarm rate?

Non-destructive detection of package- and board-level tampering.

Terms: FIB (focused ion beam): a lab tool that can cut and rewire a chip to insert edits. PDN (power distribution network): the network that carries power from the board's voltage regulator to the chip's transistors; a change in its electrical impedance can signal tampering. W D (WD): the Wasserstein distance, the paper's measure of how far a board's measured impedance has moved from its untampered reference.

Status

Started Non-destructive, measured on hardware: an on-FPGA network analyser reads the board's power-delivery-network impedance and flags an added shunt resistor, removed decoupling capacitors, a nearby EM probe and a polished package on commercial FPGA development boards, one board per tamper event, with a global threshold that gave no false alarms on the authors' own measurements. Not a detection and false-alarm rate across devices; not a data-centre GPU board; circuit edits inside the package (FIB) and re-encapsulation are not covered. Related held work: BackMon (Mosavirik & Tajik 2024) detects removal of the cooling stack exposing a flip-chip die backside, one board, no error rates; Safa et al. 2023 use VNA impedance signatures to spot aged chips. The headline is not answered within a stated scope.

The magnitude of the WD metric shows that all tamper events performed in this paper can be distinguished if we use the sensor at the right frequency. Moreover, the detection thresholds for different tamper events, and their corresponding frequency bands, can be selected differently. However, we still can define a global threshold at W D = 3 for the tampering experiments presented in this work without causing any false alarms.
Mosavirik, Schaumont & Tajik (2022), ImpedanceVerif: On-Chip Impedance Sensing for System-Level Tampering Detection (IACR TCHES 2023, no. 1) (2022), L829–833
