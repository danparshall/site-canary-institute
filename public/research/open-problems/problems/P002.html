P002 Tamper-respondent enclosures

Can a tamper-respondent enclosure for a high-power accelerator pass liquid cooling and full interconnect while erasing its keys on intrusion, at a low false-trigger rate and a hard-to-defeat threshold?

Tamper-respondent enclosures for accelerators: red-teams, cooling, zeroisation, false triggers.

Terms: FIPS 140 Level 4: the highest tamper-resistance tier in the US/Canada standard for cryptographic hardware, requiring an enclosure that detects and responds to physical intrusion.

Status

Analyzed Design options only, for enclosures around accelerators dissipating up to 1,200 W: active sensing by capacitance, resistance mesh, radio response or distributed sensors; response by key deletion or permanent disablement (capacitor discharge, antifuses, nanothermite); cooling pass-through by a thin boundary under the cold plate, a contained liquid loop with a boundary heat exchanger, or metalised foam for air; enclosure sizes from chip to rack. Nothing built at this scale, no red team, no false-trigger rate; the authors judge that nation-state attackers can likely compromise the best current enclosures and that the per-device cost of circumvention is hard to estimate. The sensing prototypes the section cites are not held. Cross-references, not evidence for this headline: Immler et al. (2019) built and attacked a PCB-scale PUF cover (enclosure PUFs problem); Amodo's tray-level sensor retrofit with power-down response (seals and sensor suites problem) is not an enclosure.

Secure enclosures can ideally be made compatible with both air and direct to chip liquid cooling … To permit cooling, the secure enclosure boundary can either be sandwiched between the chip face and cooling pad, or it can contain both the chip and the cooling pad. If it is sandwiched beneath the cooling pad, it has to be very thin and very thermally conductive.
Petrie & Aarne (2025), Technical Options for Flexible Hardware-Enabled Guarantees (flexHEG Part 2) (2025-04), L935–941
