P020 Side-channel defences at scale

Can side-channel defences (shielding, filters, jamming, wardens) be mass-manufactured and retrofitted to cap a tray's leakage below a stated level, and at what unit cost?

Engineering side-channel defences (TEMPEST, filters, jamming, wardens) at scale.

Terms: TEMPEST: the standard body of techniques for shielding equipment so it does not leak information through stray electromagnetic emissions. wardens: monitors that watch for such leakage.

Status

Analyzed A paper design from a research sprint the author calls 'a first-pass viability study': enclosures, sensors, look-through jamming, inspections and physical security with an estimated bill of materials below 1% of cluster cost (this survey's cost goal of 1% of facility cost, met on paper only); power-filter volume cost 'has proven difficult' to pin down; no attenuation or leakage-rate cap measured; the 39 dB look-through suppression is cited from commodity radios in work not held; wardens: no work ('unexplored').

I present an example of a defense architecture towards the end of this post. It leverages a synergy of cheap enclosures, sensors, jamming, unannounced hardware inspections and physical security … at an estimated (!) Bill-of-Materials (BOM) below 1% of the cost of the monitored cluster
Cankaya (2026), Suppressing Side Channels in an Untrusted Data Center via Retrofitted Defenses (MIRI TGT blog) (2026-05-29), L62–65
