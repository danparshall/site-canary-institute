P019 Bounding compute from power and analog measurement

How tightly can power and analog observables bound a chip's or facility's compute, and how much does the bound loosen from on-die to feed meter under adversarial choice?

Power-side compute bounds (beta*, energy per op) and the chip/node analog observables and meter limits that feed them, from on-die to feed meter.

Terms: β (beta): the largest hidden computation a power trace cannot rule out, as a fraction of the declared machine capacity. It depends on declared utilisation (how busy the declared work keeps the chip); β (beta) is its worst case over all utilisations. The paper's headline "β = 1.16" is that worst case; 0.41 is what a demonstrated attack actually hid. Energy per operation: joules consumed per arithmetic operation, used to turn power readings into a compute estimate. ΔP0 (delta-P0): the spread of the chip's non-compute overhead power (idle draw, cooling, I/O), which the verifier cannot see separately from compute; 41.2 W on the A100 measured. w_C: how much energy per operation varies with where the data sits (the 'locality' factor; 1.04× on the A100 measured). N: the number of devices.

Status

Started One NVIDIA A100 model, one driver, one cluster; energy read from on-die telemetry with a perfect meter assumed; the authors' own adversary model; the bound tightens to about 0.06 only with re-execution at an observed operating point; a 0.41-1.16 gap left open. The loosening from on-die to feed meter and the multi-device facility bound are explicitly not addressed ('assume a perfect meter'; 'do not offer a facility-level, multi-device version'), so that clause has no work.

Measurements on NVIDIA A100 GPUs constrain β = 1.16 in the worst case, while adversarial matched-energy strategies are shown to hide at least β = 0.41 of compute.
Kimpson, Baker & Graham (2026), Can Power Draw Constrain Covert Compute? Limits of Analogue Verification for AI Governance (arXiv 2610.07476v1) (2026-10-05), L19–22
