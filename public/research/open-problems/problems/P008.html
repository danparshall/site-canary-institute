P008 Chain of custody from fab to packaging

Can a custody scheme detect a wafer or die that goes missing or is swapped between fab and packaging, and identify who last held it?

Die-level identity and records (ECID, tamper-evident die-ID commitments, tool-signed process records) plus sealed transport, detecting a missing or substituted die between fab and packaging.

Terms: ECID (electronic chip ID): a unique serial number burned into each die, readable electronically over the IEEE 1149.1 (JTAG) test interface. Merkle root: a single short fingerprint summarising a whole set of records, so any later change to any record is detectable.

Status

Analyzed A proposed custody principle only: verifiable transport at each step from fab through dicing and packaging, with liability on the last holder; no mechanism, miss rate or test. Cankaya (2025) describes signed ECID handoffs second-hand from Zhang & Guin (2019), which is not held, and leaves 'who last had custody' for a later post; Scher & Thiergart list CISA-style chain-of-custody documentation as a building block. Nothing deployed for an AI-accelerator supply chain.

it is important for the wafers (and later the dies) to be securely and verifiably transported at each step. If these chip precursors ever go missing, responsibility for the violation would lie with the most recent holder.
Shavit (2023), What Does It Take to Catch a Chinchilla? Verifying Rules on Large-Scale Neural Network Training via Compute Monitoring (2023-05-30), L907–909
