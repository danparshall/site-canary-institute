P059 GPU confidential computing and link encryption: availability and overhead

Which shipping accelerators support multi-GPU confidential computing and encrypted links, at what throughput cost and with what key handling?

Which accelerators ship multi-GPU confidential computing / encrypted NVLink and links, at what throughput cost, with what binding and key handling.

Terms: confidential computing: keeping data encrypted even while a chip is processing it. NVLink: Nvidia's high-speed link between GPUs. AES-GCM: a standard method for encrypting and authenticating data in transit.

Status

Started Measured on one Huawei Ascend 910A accelerator, a vendor research prototype of firmware-only confidential computing: 0.028-0.91% added inference latency. Single device and inference only; no multi-device or training overhead has been published. A workshop participant reports an unpublished characterisation of training overhead from 2 to 1,024 GPUs; it is not counted, since no numbers are public. Availability: in January 2026 only the H100 offered working confidential computing; cluster-scale confidential computing on Blackwell at near-unencrypted speed is a vendor claim. Link encryption: roughly 3% of compute to encrypt NVLink traffic is a desk estimate.

introduces minimal performance loss during the inference pass(0.91% in GPT2-neo125M and 0.028% in Llama3-Chat-QA-1.5-8B model
Dhar et al. (2024), Ascend-CC: Confidential Computing on Heterogeneous NPU for Emerging Generative AI Workloads (Huawei) (2024), L140
