P116 International verification body: powers and governance

What powers, governance, staffing and access should an international AI verification body have?

The powers, governance, staffing, inspector model, access and registry governance of an international AI verification body.

Status

Started An empirical result from the Alva Myrdal Centre Arms Control Agreement Database (99 compliance mechanisms, 33 treaties, 1959 to 2015) on one leg only, the inspector model. Powers, decision-making, staffing, trigger calibration, access provisions and registry governance have paper proposals at most (the authors' three-layer inspectorate; Harack et al. and Scher treat body design as political and out of scope), and the authors call them 'the substantive work ahead'.

The scope of the agreement is strongly associated with the inspector model. Bilateral treaties use party-on-party inspectors 95% of the time; multilateral treaties use institutional inspectors 47.4% of the time, roughly 10× the bilateral rate of 4.9%.
Leklem & Maheshwari (2026), How Should We 'Inspect' Artificial Intelligence? (Alva Myrdal Centre insights) (2026-06-16), L80–83
