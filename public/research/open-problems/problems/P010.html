P010 Chip identity and fingerprinting

Is there a clone-resistant chip or board identifier, or a fingerprint that tells apart individual chips of the same model, with a published false-match rate and cost that a prover cannot defeat by swapping in another chip?

Clone-resistant chip/board identifiers and same-SKU die fingerprints.

Terms: same-SKU die: an individual chip of the same product model as others (SKU = a product/stock code). PUF (physically unclonable function): a chip's unique, hard-to-copy fingerprint arising from tiny manufacturing variations. S-parameters: radio-frequency measurements of a board, usable as such a fingerprint.

Status

Started Software (atomic-race) fingerprint telling apart 24 same-model H200 GPUs rented on vast.ai, 480 runs; up to 100% paired re-identification. The authors' own limitations: not validated at larger scale; stability over time, transport and temperature not established; security against an adversary who emulates the GPU or runs the function faster not tested, so the headline's 'cannot defeat by swapping in another chip' clause and a published false-match rate at scale remain open. Lower-ranked evidence: Alpay & Alpay (2026) measure a per-SM latency map stable over a six-hour RTX 5090 run and 100% leave-one-out separation across distinct Blackwell dies, but leave same-model die separation to prior work; Safa, Mosavirik & Tajik (2023) distinguish an aged STM32 from 10–12 genuine ones by S-parameters with no error rates. No cost figure in any held source.

we evaluated the function across 24 Nvidia H200 GPUs, 2 seeds, and 10 runs each … fingerprinting function that achieved up to 100% accuracy in small-scale tests
