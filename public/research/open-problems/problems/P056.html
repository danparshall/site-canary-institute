P056 Secure boot and firmware integrity

Can an untrusting verifier confirm an accelerator runs only signed, non-rolled-back, auditable firmware, and has secure boot been bypassed in published work?

Secure boot, anti-rollback and auditability of accelerator firmware.

Status

Analyzed Design options only: jointly created firmware reference measurements, and listed ways to attest that verification firmware stays present (secure boot made impossible to disable, a secure counter, randomly timed low-latency attestation); Shavit 2023 proposes signed-firmware enforcement, anti-rollback and early remote attestation of updates. Nothing built or tested. Bypass clause at a lower stage: no held document reports a secure-boot bypass of a data-centre GPU; flexHEG Part 2 describes voltage-glitch attacks on secure boot in general from a source not held; Skorobogatov & Woods 2012 broke an FPGA's protections, not an accelerator's. H100 secure boot and anti-rollback features are reported second-hand (Petrie 2024; Scher & Thiergart) from NVIDIA documents not held.

To obtain reliable reference measurements when the hardware designer’s measurements are untrusted, without gaining access to private information such as the Prover’s code, one option may be for the Verifier and Prover to jointly create reference measurements by emulating a compliant bootup on a confidentiality-preserving technology (e.g., Confidential Computing).
Baker, Kulp, Marks, Brundage & Heim (2025), Verifying International Agreements on AI: Six Layers of Verification (RAND WR-A4077-1) (2025-07-25), L2403–2407
