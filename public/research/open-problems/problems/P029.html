P029 Passive optical taps at high lane rates

Can a passive optical tap and monitor receiver recover a high-speed optical lane within its link budget while keeping the live link error-free?

Passive optical taps and monitor receivers at 53-112 GBaud.

Terms: GBaud (gigabaud): billions of signal symbols per second. PAM4: a scheme sending two bits per symbol using four levels. FEC (forward error correction): extra data that lets a receiver fix errors. DR4/DR8: standard multi-lane optical link types.

Status

Analyzed Desk link-budget analysis for the live leg: baseline optical margins against tap insertion loss, with feasibility thresholds of 1.5 dB and 0.5 dB, for 400G-class multimode links inside a scalable unit and single-mode links between units; the monitor-leg recovery at 53-112 GBaud is left as the harder problem and the author says amplification is probably indispensable at 112 GBaud. No tap built or measured at this problem's lane rates. Milton & Reynolds (Amodo, 2026) reach a similar desk result ('even a 1 dB loss may be unacceptable') and say they plan testing; commercial passive taps exist for 26 GBaud lanes (400G-DR8), below this problem's lane rates, which are the headline's stated difficulty, so that is a cross-reference and not evidence; vendors' statements that 53 GBaud should work are unverified hearsay. Cankaya's fleet-margin argument cites Meta production telemetry (John et al., DTS 2025), not held.

Intra-SU multi-mode: Passive taps feasible only at 95/5 with direct cabling. Infeasible with standard structured cabling.
Cankaya (2026), Research Note: The Fundamentals and Feasibility of Secure Network Taps for Verifying AI Datacenter Use (Substack), appendix B.5 link-budget feasibility (2026-04-30 (live page read 2026-10-21)), L288
