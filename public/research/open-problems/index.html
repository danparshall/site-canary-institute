Open problems in AI compute verification

Review draft, not yet announced. Comments and corrections are welcome: dan@canaryinstitute.ai.

127 problem areas that still need work before verifying AI compute is a reliable technology, drawn from 52 documents and grouped by the outside fields most likely to answer them. Most problems show a quote stating the problem, checked against the source by two independent readers; other documents that raise a problem are listed without quotes. Each problem has its own page, listing its sources, its checkable sub-questions (723 in all) and those not yet stated precisely enough to check (283); a further 10 sub-questions without a source are in the data download only.

This is my best understanding as of 2026-10-27. It does not claim that nobody is working on these problems. The list is not complete; problems found since it was drawn up will be added. Corrections are welcome: dan@canaryinstitute.ai.

Problem titles, questions, statements and sub-questions are our paraphrases; only text in quotation marks is the sources' own. A number in a sub-question either is one of the numbers listed below, cites its source, or is marked as set by this survey; a value not yet set is marked “to be agreed”.

A status describes the most advanced public work we found in the documents we hold that state these problems. We have not yet searched the wider literature for solutions, so a Posed or Analyzed problem may have work we have not seen. Based on multi-agent and manual review, we expect roughly 10% of items to change their status upon closer inspection. Corrections are welcome: dan@canaryinstitute.ai.

  • Posed a document states the problem, and no public document we hold reports work on it.
  • Analyzed a design, protocol, analysis, model or simulation exists, but nothing has been built, run on hardware, or run on real data.
  • Started something built, measured or run on real data bears on the question, but does not yet answer it.
  • Done a public document answers every part of the question within a stated scope.
  • Failed a test, proof or systematic assessment shows that a named family of mechanisms does not work.

Numbers used in the sub-questions

Where a sub-question uses one of these numbers, it takes it from the source given here.

  • Run size: 10^24 FLOP, the "Strict Threshold" above which training runs are prohibited in MIRI's draft international agreement (Scher, Abeyta et al. 2025, Article II). Sub-questions about larger runs keep their own numbers.
  • Chip line: 16 H100-equivalents: the agreement's "covered chip cluster" line. This survey's island-size study finds that what the line costs an evader depends strongly on how the cost is measured.
  • Detection goal: Detect a covert accumulation of 10^24 FLOP with at least 90% probability before it is complete. Like the IAEA's inspection goal, this applies to the regime's checks over a period taken together; a single check or component can be much weaker. It assumes a cheater's gain is at most nine times the sanction (Avenhaus & Canty 1996).

P002AnalyzedTamper-respondent enclosuresCan a tamper-respondent enclosure for a high-power accelerator pass liquid cooling and full interconnect while erasing its keys on intrusion, at a low false-trigger rate and a hard-to-defeat threshold?9

Status

Analyzed Design options only, for enclosures around accelerators dissipating up to 1,200 W: active sensing by capacitance, resistance mesh, radio response or distributed sensors; response by key deletion or permanent disablement (capacitor discharge, antifuses, nanothermite); cooling pass-through by a thin boundary under the cold plate, a contained liquid loop with a boundary heat exchanger, or metalised foam for air; enclosure sizes from chip to rack. Status

Started Non-destructive, measured on hardware: an on-FPGA network analyser reads the board's power-delivery-network impedance and flags an added shunt resistor, removed decoupling capacitors, a nearby EM probe and a polished package on commercial FPGA development boards, one board per tamper event, with a global threshold that gave no false alarms on the authors' own measurements.
