Two sides with their own estimates

Axes are each side's own hazard estimate. Shading: mutual cooperation is selected where qA* + qB* < 1 (darker = more margin). Both sides share the sliders above except the four here. Hatched: a side with no region where cooperating is dominant, so selection is not guaranteed there.

Model: analysis/race_game/race_model.py (checked in check_derivations.py; the JS below is tested against it on a grid). Papenhausen's main widget is the stop world with θ = 0, |Vl| = 1, no verification; his appendix adds θ (his α; θ here for Greek thrasos, reckless building): the frontier project, completed first, escapes control with probability H; if it stays under control, the laggard's does with probability θ·H. Roussel et al. 2026 is the slow-down world with their "pause" as Slowed, hazard 1 − s = 0.15 and lag 0.15; the y axis is then their C. RAND (Abraham, Kavner & Moon 2025) is not a preset: their shared cost c is paid in full whenever anyone accelerates, while here a lone builder's catastrophe cost scales with its win probability, so one cell differs. In the stop world the resumption slider sets UPP as a share of the resumed (S,S) race. Under verification a side that discovers a cheat (caught covertly, or an open breakout) chooses the better of keeping its action and racing from behind; a known defector, caught covertly or breaking out openly, pays the defection punishment, in safe wins (whether it would really be applied is folded into ε for now). The deterrence line in the payoff panel is the smallest punishment at which mutual cooperation becomes an equilibrium at the chosen point. Finite-noise cutoffs use uniform noise, a flat prior and a 0.25% grid in H.
